## New Product Pricing Strategy

- Product prices at the introductory stage are challenging
- Two strategies:  market-skimming pricing and market-penetration pricing

### Market- Skimming pricing

- Set initial high prices to skim revenues layer by layer from the market
- Being by charging high prices for customers who are willing to pay the most 
- Lower prices over time
- **Market-skimming pricing:** Setting high price for new product to skim maximum profits layer by layer from customers segments in line with their willingness to pay
- Makes sense if products quality and image must support higher price, enough buyers must want the product at that price. Product must be differentiated so competitors can not undercut, brand must be sufficiently strong so price-conscious consumers will wait for lower prices instead of buying from competitors. Cost of producing a small file cannot be high enough that they cancel advantage of charging more

### Market-Penetration Pricing

- **Market-penetration pricing:** Setting low price for a new product in order to quickly attract buyers and gain a large market share
- Conditions to be met
  - Market must be highly price sensitive so low price produces more market growth
  - Production and distribution costs decrease as sales decrease as sales volume increases
  - Low price must help keep out competition
  - Must be able to maintain low-price position
  - Market share must maintain more profitable revenue streams long term

## Product Mix Pricing Strategies

- Product pricing is often changed when a product is part of a product mix
- Pricing is difficult because various products have related demand demand and face different degrees of competition

### Product line pricing

- **Product line pricing:** Setting the prices across various products in a produce line based on cost differences between products, evaluations of different features, and competitor pricing

### Optional-product Pricing

- **Optional-product-pricing:** pricing of optional or accessory products along with a main product
- Companies choose what to include in the base price and what to offer as options

### Captive-product pricing

- **Captive-product-pricing:** Setting a price for product that must be used along with a main product
  - Ex: toner cartridges, Kindle, Fire Tablets,etc.
- Fixed-fee plus a variable usage rate

### By-product pricing

- **By-product pricing:** Setting a price for and selling by-products to improve profits and to offset costs of disposing 

### Product Bundle Pricing

- **Product bundle pricing:** Combining several products and offering bundle at reduced price

## Price Adjustment Strategies

### Discount and Allowance pricing:

- **Discount:** straight reduction in price on purchases made during stated period of time or in larger quantities
  - Quantity discount- price reduction for large volumes
  - Functional discount (trade discount) - Discount to trade channel members
  - Seasonal Discount - discount in a specific season
- **Allowance:** price reduction given to customers who participate in a certain program

### Segmented Pricing

- **Segmented pricing:** Selling a product of service at two or more prices, where change in price is not linked to change in cost 
  - Product form pricing, Location-based pricing

### Psychological Pricing

- **Psychological pricing:** Considers psychology and not just economics behind how consumers evaluate pricing
- **Reference pricing:** Prices that buyers carry in mind when referring to a specific product

### Promotional pricing

- **Promotional pricing:** Temporarily pricing products below list price, sometimes below cost to increase short-run sales
  - Flash sales, limited time offers, special-event pricing

### Geographical Pricing

- **Geographical pricing:** Setting prices for customers located in different parts of the world
- **FOB-origin pricing:** Pricing in which goods are placed free on board a carrier, customer pays freight from factory to destination
- **Uniform-delivered:** (Opposite of FOB): Pricing in which the company charges same delivered price, including freight to all customers 
- **Zone pricing:** Pricing with 2 or more delivery zones, all customers in a zone have the same price
- **Basing-point pricing:** Pricing in which seller designates some city as base point, charges customer cost from city to customer
- **Freight-absorption pricing:** Pricing in which seller absorbs all or part of freight charges

### Dynamic and Personalized Pricing

- **Fixed-price:** setting one price for all buyers
- **Dynamic pricing:** Adjusting prices continually in real time to meet changing conditions and situations in marketplace
- **Personalized pricing:** adjusting in real time to fit customer needs, situations, locations and buying behaviors

## Price Changes

### Initiating price cuts

- Price premium is most valuable assets a company can posses
- Increases in prices might make consumers believe that the company is price-gouging
- Many states have anti-gouging laws

### Buyer reaction to price changes

- Price increase could mean that the new product offers some features, or could be seen as being greedy
- Cut price might mean that quality has been reduced, or product isn't selling well and brand image might be tarnished

### Competitor reactions to price changes

- Competitors are most likely to react when there are a small number of competitors
- Can be difficult to determine competitors reactions to price changes
  - Must assess competitors reactions

### Responding to Price Changes

- Several issues:
  - Why did they change the price?
  - Is the change to be temporary or permanent?
  - What will happen to market share or profits?
  - Consider situation and strategy and possible customer reactions to price changes.

## Public Policy and Pricing

- Price competition is a core element of our free-market economy
- Companies must consider broader societal pricing concerns
- Most important pieces of legislation: Sherman Act, Clayton Act, Robinson-Patman Act

### Pricing within Channel levels

- Sellers must set prices without talking to competitors, otherwise price-collusion is suspected
- **Predatory pricing:** selling below cost with the intention of punishing a competitor or gaining higher long-run profits by putting competitors out of business

### Pricing across Channel Levels

- Robinson-Patman Act seeks to prevent unfair price discrimination by ensuring sellers offer same price terms to customers at given level of trade
  - Allowed if seller can prove that its cost are different when selling to different retailers
- **Retail (or resale) price maintenance:** manufacturer cannot require dealers to charge specified retail price for product or competitors products
- **Deceptive pricing:** occurs when seller states prices or price savings that mislead consumers or are not actually available to consumer
- **Scanner fraud:** increasing complaints on overcharging on scanner checkouts
- Treating customers fairly and understanding terms helps to build customer loyalty
