### Chapter 5: Consumer Markets and Buyer Behavior

* **The Black Box:** Consumer behavior revolves around how external stimuli (like marketing or economic factors) enter the buyer's "black box" and translate into observable purchase responses.
* **Four Major Influences:** Consumer choices are constantly shaped by Cultural (core values, social class), Social (family, reference groups), Personal (age, occupation, lifestyle), and Psychological (motivation, perception, beliefs) factors.
* **Buying Decision Types:** Purchasing behavior varies based on consumer involvement and brand differences, categorizing decisions as Complex, Dissonance-Reducing, Habitual, or Variety-Seeking.
* **The Decision Process:** When making major purchases, consumers generally go through Need Recognition, Information Search, Evaluation of Alternatives, Purchase Decision, and finally, Postpurchase Behavior.

### Chapter 6: B2B Buyer Behavior

* **B2B vs. Consumer Markets:** Business markets deal with fewer but larger buyers, rely on "derived demand" (stemming directly from consumer trends), and involve much more complex, formalized purchasing processes.
* **The Buying Center:** B2B purchasing decisions rarely involve just one person; they rely on Users, Influencers, Buyers, Deciders, and Gatekeepers (who control the flow of information).
* **Types of Buying Situations:** Organizations categorize purchases as a Straight Rebuy (routine restocking), a Modified Rebuy (changing specs or vendors), or a New Task (a highly complex, first-time purchase).
* **The 8-Step Decision Process:** B2B buying is rigorous, starting with Problem Recognition and concluding with a Performance Review that dictates the future of the supplier relationship.
* **Digital B2B Marketing:** While e-procurement greatly lowers costs and speeds up the buying cycle, it can erode relationships; the best solution is a "Digital + Human Hybrid Approach" that reserves human interaction for complex needs.
* **Specialized Markets:** Institutional markets (like schools or hospitals) are characterized by low budgets and captive patrons, while government markets require strict compliance and formal bidding processes.
