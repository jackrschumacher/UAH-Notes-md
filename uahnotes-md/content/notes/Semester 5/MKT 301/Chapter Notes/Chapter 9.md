# Summary of Chapter 9: Developing New Products and Managing the Product Life Cycle

This presentation outlines how companies create and manage new products from initial ideation through their eventual decline. Companies typically obtain new products in two ways: through acquisition (buying a company, patent, or license) or through their own internal new product development.   

## The New Product Development (NPD) Process

The development of original products, improvements, or modifications follows a systematic, eight-step process:   

- **Idea Generation:** The systematic search for new product ideas using internal sources (R&D, staff), external sources (customers, competitors), and crowdsourcing.   
- **Idea Screening:** Identifying good ideas and dropping poor ones using the "R-W-W" framework: Is it real? Can we win? Is it worth doing?.   
- **Concept Development and Testing:** Translating a product idea into a detailed product concept and testing it with groups of target consumers.   
- **Marketing Strategy Development:** Designing an initial marketing strategy that outlines the target market, value proposition, sales goals, and marketing mix.   
- **Business Analysis:** Reviewing sales, costs, and profit projections to ensure they meet company objectives.   
- **Product Development:** Transforming the product concept into a physical, workable market offering.   
- **Test Marketing:** Testing the physical product and its proposed marketing program in realistic market settings before a full launch.   
- **Commercialization:** Officially introducing the new product into the market, which involves deciding when and where to launch.   

For new product development to be successful, the approach must be customer-centered, team-based, and systematic.   

## The Product Life Cycle (PLC)

Once a product is launched, it moves through distinct stages, each requiring different marketing strategies:   

- **Product Development:** Sales are at zero while investment costs increase as the product is being created.   
- **Introduction:** Sales grow slowly, distribution and promotion expenses are high, and there are little to no profits.   
- **Growth:** The product experiences rapid market acceptance and increasing profits. Competitors enter the market, economies of scale are achieved, and prices may be lowered to attract more buyers.   
- **Maturity:** Sales growth slows down and profits level off or decline. The market sees overcapacity, substitute products, and increased competition, leading companies to try modifying the market, product, or marketing mix to support sales.   
- **Decline:** Sales fall off significantly and profits drop.   

The presentation also notes that special life cycle patterns exist for broad styles, specific fashions, and fast-peaking fads.   

## The Role of AI and Additional Considerations

Artificial Intelligence is increasingly utilized in product development. AI can help find and develop new product ideas, streamline the overall development process, effectively manage the product lifecycle, and enhance socially responsible product ideas. Finally, marketers must consider public policy, international marketing challenges, and regulations regarding product safety, quality, and patents throughout the product's life.
