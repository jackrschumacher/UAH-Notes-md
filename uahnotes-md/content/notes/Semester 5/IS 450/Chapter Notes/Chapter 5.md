## Ethics & Professional Conduct

- Cybersecurity professionals require high ethical standards because they are entrusted with sensitive organizational information and design systems that institutionalize behavioral policies.   
- Unethical behavior is typically categorized into three areas: ignorance, accident, and intent.   
- Organizations use laws, policies, and technical controls as deterrents, but these only work if there is a fear of a penalty, a probability of being caught, and a probability of the penalty being administered.   
- Major professional organizations enforce strict codes of conduct:
  - **(ISC)²:** Enforces four mandatory canons: protect society, act honorably, provide diligent service, and advance the profession.   
  - **SANS (GIAC):** Requires respect for the public, the certification, the employer, and oneself.   
  - **ACM, ISACA, & ISSA:** Promote high ethical computing standards, management practices ensuring confidentiality, integrity, and availability, and proper auditing controls.   

## Key U.S. Cybersecurity & Privacy Laws

- **Computer Fraud and Abuse (CFA) Act (1986):** The cornerstone of federal computer law; carries penalties up to 20 years in prison for offenses tied to financial gain or criminal acts. It was later expanded by the **USA PATRIOT Act (2001)** to combat terrorism-related activities.   
- **Computer Security Act (CSA) (1987):** Established minimum security practices for federal computer systems and mandated periodic security awareness training.   
- **Privacy & Electronic Data Laws:**
  - **Privacy Act (1974):** Regulates how government agencies use and release private information.   
  - **Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA) (1986):** Also known as federal wiretapping acts; regulates the interception of electronic/oral communications in cooperation with the Fourth Amendment.   
  - **HIPAA (1996) & HITECH (2009):** HIPAA establishes five privacy principles for health care data security, while HITECH strictly mandates breach notifications to information owners.   
  - **Gramm-Leach-Bliley (GLB) Act (1999):** Requires financial institutions to disclose privacy policies and allow customers to opt out of third-party data sharing.   
- **Corporate & Intellectual Property Laws:**
  - **Sarbanes-Oxley (SOX) Act (2002):** Enforces strict accountability for financial record keeping at publicly traded corporations.   
  - **Economic Espionage Act (EEA) (1996):** Protects trade secrets, intellectual property, and competitive advantages.   

## International Laws & Industry Standards

- **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) (2018):** Strict EU law applying to any organization handling EU/EEA residents' data, standardizing protections and enhancing individual control.   
- **European Council Cybercrime Convention:** An international task force built to standardize technology laws and improve cybercrime investigations.   
- **Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS):** An industry-mandated standard (currently version 4.0) to reduce credit card fraud, featuring 12 requirements across six areas: secure networks, account data protection, vulnerability management, access control, monitoring/testing, and information security policy maintenance.   

## Policy Management & Organizational Liability

- **Policy vs. Law:** While ignorance of the law is never an excuse, ignorance of policy *is* a viable defense. Therefore, policies must be universally distributed, read, understood (including multilingual support), formally acknowledged (via consent forms), and uniformly enforced.   
- **Liability:** Organizations reduce their liability by demonstrating **due care** (ensuring employees know what actions are acceptable/unacceptable) and **due diligence** (making valid, ongoing efforts to protect others).   
- **Law Enforcement Collaboration:** Security professionals may interact with the **FBI** (via the InfraGard partnership program), the **NSA's Information Assurance Directorate (IAD)** (for cyber defense solutions), or the **U.S. Secret Service** (which investigates computer fraud and false identification crimes).
