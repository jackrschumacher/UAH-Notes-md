
## Risk Management Foundations & Frameworks

- Risk management identifies, assesses, and mitigates risks to information assets, addressing four core questions: identification, analysis, evaluation, and treatment.
- The overall strategic structure is guided by frameworks such as the **NIST RMF**, which consists of seven steps: Prepare, Categorize, Select, Implement, Assess, Authorize, and Monitor.
- The **DoW Cybersecurity Risk Management Construct (CSRMC)** modernizes legacy risk management by transitioning to dynamic, continuous cyber defense. This construct operates across five phases: Design, Build, Test, Onboard, and Operations.

## Risk Identification Process

- Organizations must systematically identify, classify, categorize, and prioritize their information assets based on their overall importance.
- Managers assess potential weaknesses against 12 established threat categories, which include software attacks, espionage, human error, and information extortion.
- Vulnerabilities are documented by comparing these prioritized assets and threats, ultimately culminating in the creation of a Threats-Vulnerabilities-Assets (TVA) worksheet.

## Risk Analysis, Evaluation & Treatment

- Risk analysis assigns a relative score to each vulnerability using the formula: `Risk = Likelihood x Impact`. This calculation sometimes incorporates an element of uncertainty.
- During risk evaluation, these calculated risk ratings are compared against the organization's predefined risk appetite to determine if the residual risk is acceptable.
- Unacceptable risks require risk treatment, meaning the organization must remove the asset from harm's way, modify its current protection, or transfer the protection responsibility to a third party.

## AI & Emerging Risks

- **Data and Model Security:** AI introduces new vulnerabilities such as training data poisoning, model collapse, and model inversion. These risks require mitigation strategies like data integrity monitoring, model isolation, and secure data management practices.
- **Application and Infrastructure:** AI applications are susceptible to input injection, unauthorized access, and model denial of service. Organizations must implement strict access controls, secure deployment in virtual sandboxes, and continuous perimeter hardening to reduce breach risks.
